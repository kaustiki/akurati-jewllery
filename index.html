Part A · Gold · 1

Karat: how pure the gold is

Pure gold is too soft to hold a diamond, so jewellers mix it with other metals. That mix is called an alloy. Karat says how much of the alloy is gold, counted out of 24 parts.

What changes as the karat goes up

Karat (K or KT) is about gold. Carat (ct) is about a diamond's weight. They sound the same but have nothing to do with each other. "14K gold with 8 cents of diamonds" uses both.
WordWhat it means
AlloyGold mixed with silver, copper and zinc. The mix makes the gold harder and sets its colour.
Fineness / stampPurity in parts per 1,000. 585 means 585 of every 1,000 parts are gold (14K). "916" on a shop board means 22K.
"Touch"What many South Indian jewellers call purity. "75 touch" means 18K.
Yellow, rose, white goldSame karat, different alloy. More copper makes rose gold. White gold is alloyed with white metals and usually rhodium-plated to look bright white.
Lower karatLess gold, so it is cheaper, harder and paler. It is also lighter: the same design weighs about 15% less in 14K than in 18K.

Part A · Gold · 5

Hallmark: the government's proof of purity

A BIS hallmark is tested and lasered on by an official Assaying and Hallmarking Centre, not by the jeweller. It proves the karat. BIS is the Bureau of Indian Standards.

RuleWhat it means for you
Compulsory grades14K, 18K, 20K, 22K, 23K and 24K must be hallmarked when sold by a registered jeweller. 14K is a full official grade. 9K can be hallmarked, but only voluntarily.
HUIDHallmark Unique ID: 6 letters and numbers, different for every piece. Since April 2023, hallmarked gold cannot be sold without one.
Under 2 gramsPieces under 2 g are exempt from compulsory hallmarking. That covers your nose pins and probably each Vina Rekha earring. Hallmarking them anyway builds trust.
Fee₹75 per piece + tax (raised from ₹45 in September 2026), minimum ₹200 per batch.
RegistrationJewellers who sell hallmarked gold register with BIS. Ask your CA whether Akurati needs to.

Part B · Diamonds · 6

Carat and cents: a diamond's weight

1 carat = 100 cents, the same way 1 rupee = 100 paise. A "10-cent diamond" weighs 0.10 carat. One carat is only 0.2 grams. Small stones are ordered by their width in mm, which is easier to picture.

WordMeaning
Cent / pointer1/100 of a carat. Traders also say "a 10-pointer".
MeleeTiny stones under about 0.2 ct, bought in lots. Nearly all your stones.
SolitaireOne larger stone, graded on its own. Veena's centres.
Total carat weightAll the stones in a piece added together.

Part B · Diamonds · 7

Colour: how white the diamond is

Colour is graded with letters from D (whitest) to Z (yellow). Each step down is a little more yellow. The scale starts at D because the grading system wanted a fresh start, unlike older systems that used A, B and C.

How colour is checked

GradesWhat you seeFor you
D, E, FIcy white, like clear water.EF is your choice. It is the trade's normal grade for laboratory-grown stones and costs little extra.
G, HWhite to almost everyone, especially in yellow gold.Fine, but competitors advertise EF.
I, JA slight warmth next to a whiter stone.Not needed.
K and belowVisibly yellowish.Avoid.
Why it costs less for you: for mined diamonds, D costs far more than H. For laboratory-grown diamonds the gap is small, so the whitest grades are affordable. Yellow gold also reflects a little warmth into every stone, which hides small colour differences.
Part B · Diamonds · 8

Clarity: the tiny marks inside

Nearly every diamond has tiny natural marks inside it, formed while it grew. They are called inclusions. Think of the tiny bubbles inside an ice cube. Marks on the surface are called blemishes. Clarity grades how many there are and how easily they show under a 10× magnifier.

GradeFull nameWhat it means
FL, IFFlawless, Internally FlawlessNothing inside, even at 10×. Rare and expensive.
VVS1, VVS2Very, Very Slightly IncludedAn expert struggles to find anything at 10×.
VS1, VS2Very Slightly IncludedAn expert finds small marks at 10×. Invisible to the eye.
SI1, SI2Slightly IncludedEasy to see at 10×. Sometimes visible to the eye in bigger stones.
I1, I2, I3IncludedVisible to the eye. Can dull the sparkle and weaken the stone.
Your choice: VVS to VS. On a 1 mm stone nobody can tell VVS from VS without a loupe, so do not pay extra for "VVS only" on the small stones.
Part B · Diamonds · 9

Cut and facets: where the sparkle comes from

Facets are the small flat polished faces cut on the outside of the stone, like the sides of a cut-glass tumbler. From inside, they work like mirrors. Light enters through the top, bounces off the lower facets and comes back out of the top into your eye.

The angles decide whether the light comes back

Brilliance
the white light coming back to your eye
Fire
rainbow flashes. Diamond bends light (refraction) and splits it into colours.
Scintillation
the twinkle, flashing on and off as you move
Cut grade (Excellent, Very Good, Good) checks the angles, the polish and the symmetry. 3EX means Excellent on all three. Cut is the only one of the 4Cs made by a person: the same rough stone can be cut well or badly, and cutters sometimes cut badly on purpose to keep more weight.
Part B · Diamonds · 10

Full cut and shapes

A normal round diamond has 57 or 58 facets: 33 on top and 24 or 25 underneath. That complete set is the full cut. The old single cut has only about 17 facets, one simple ring, because it is quicker to polish on very tiny stones.

Ask for full cut on every stone. Full-cut tiny stones sparkle like small stars; single-cut stones give a flat glint. Full cut is now normal for laboratory-grown melee.

Shape is not the same as cut

Shape is the outline seen from the top. Cut grade is how well the facets are angled. Any shape can be cut well or badly.

Part B · Diamonds · 11

Laboratory-grown diamonds: CVD and HPHT

A laboratory-grown diamond is diamond: the same carbon crystal, the same hardness and the same sparkle as a mined one, grown in a factory instead of dug from the ground. Both methods start from a thin slice of diamond called a seed.

HPHTCVD
Best forSmall stones. Most melee is HPHT.Bigger stones. Surat mostly grows this way.
Colour as grownUsually very white straight away.Often slightly brownish.
After growingUsually nothing more.Often heat-treated to whiten it. Allowed, but it must be declared.
Typical marksTiny metal specks from the molten metal.Tiny dark carbon pinpoints.
For your customerNo visible difference.No visible difference.
For you: accept both. Your small stones will most likely be HPHT and Veena's centres CVD. The certificate states the method and any treatment.
How to say it (IS 19469:2025): write "laboratory-grown diamond" in full, on signs, tags, bills and posts. Do not use "LGD", "lab diamond", "natural", "real", "genuine", "mined", "conflict-free", "earth-friendly", "pure" or "cultured", and never claim it equals a natural diamond.
Part B · Diamonds · 12

Certificates: proof of the diamonds

A certificate (also called a grading report) comes from a private lab that has examined the stones. It is separate from the gold hallmark.

LabWho they areFor you
IGIInternational Gemological Institute. The best known for laboratory-grown diamonds in India.Use for Veena's two centre stones.
SGLSolitaire Gemological Laboratories, an Indian lab widely used for jewellery. Cheaper.Good for certificates of the small-stone pieces.
GIAGemological Institute of America. Mostly used for mined diamonds.Not needed.
TypeWhat it covers
Stone reportOne stone: its exact carat, colour, clarity, cut, measurements, growth method and any treatment. For solitaires.
Jewellery certificateThe finished piece: total carat, colour and clarity range, gold karat and weight, and a photo. For pieces with many small stones.
Laser inscription: the report number is lasered on the stone's thin edge (the girdle). With a loupe anyone can match the stone to its certificate. IGI numbers for laboratory-grown stones start with "LG".

What a jewellery certificate shows

Your plan: an IGI stone report and inscription for each Veena centre stone, and an SGL or IGI jewellery certificate for every finished piece. Leave the details to the jeweller, but ask what each costs.
